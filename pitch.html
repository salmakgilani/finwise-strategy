Strategy · M1

The growth bet

Hypothesis

The core belief

FinWise's biggest growth problem is that trial-to-paid conversion is stuck at ~2%, because using the product in the trial isn't turning into a reason to pay.
The bet

Where I focus

Trial → paid: get trial users to the action that predicts paying in their first week, to lift conversion from 2% to 3%.

Not doing: pricing changes, more acquisition spend, new premium features.

Loop type: Collaboration, user-invites-user
Activation · M2

The Aha moment

Aha moment

When value lands

The first time a trial user gets a cash answer from their own data, like "Yes, Friday's payroll is covered."
Onboarding

4 screens to Aha

1. Pick one question (payroll, hiring, or runway)
2. Connect bank or accounting software
3. Confirm the pre-filled recurring items
4. See the answer, then set a Monday review
Retention · M3

The habit mechanic

Mechanic

Progress Bar, a forecast confidence meter

Trigger: Monday notification when new transactions come in.
Action: Import the latest transactions and review the cash forecast.
Reward: Confidence moves up (Medium → High) with an answer like "6 weeks of runway, payroll is covered."
Investment: Add upcoming invoices or a planned hire, or invite their accountant.
Rationale

Why it retains

This addresses value-related churn: modeling usage went up but sessions dropped from 11.5 to 9.3 min and churn rose, so users try the forecast but don't get an answer they trust. A user about to churn would stay because each week the forecast gets more accurate and answers a real question, which they'd lose at the end of the reverse trial.
Metrics · M4

What I measure

Data pattern

What it revealed

From Module 1: conversion is stuck at ~2%, and the biggest drop-off is the Revenue stage (98% of trials don't convert).

Did import usage correlate with conversion? No (r = −0.05). Revised my hypothesis: conversion isn't dropping and users do find the features.

What it reveals: Users are more active but sessions got shorter and conversion didn't move, so the problem is value, not engagement.

Experiment · M5

How I test it

Method

Test design

Method: A/B test

Why: Onboarding can be randomized per user, and ~490 trials a month gives a clear result in about 8 weeks.
Hypothesis

Metric + success

If we replace the current onboarding with the 4-screen answer-first flow, more new trial users will import data and reach their first modeling output within 7 days.

Primary metric: % of new trial users who import data and reach their first modeling output within 7 days, target: +10 points over control
Guardrails

Limits + read date

Guardrail: Onboarding completion doesn't drop more than 5 points vs. control.

Read date: 8 weeks after launch
Monetization · M6

The model

Stage 1 · Value Creation · Freemium
Stage + model

Where FinWise is

Stage: Stage 1 · Value Creation, trial-to-paid flat at 1.87–2.08% for 13 months while modeling usage rose to 58% and sessions dropped from 12.1 to 5.1 min

Model: Freemium, gives up some short-term revenue but keeps the 98% of trial users we currently lose
Recommendation

The move

Keep the reverse trial for new trial users, then drop them to a free tier that shows cash today and gates the weekly cash forecast (payroll and hiring answers). Targets the M4 indicator "% of trial signups who import data in session 1".

The pricing bet: Packaging matched to value
Model signal

What success looks like

Conversion stayed flat while usage went up, so value isn't proven yet and the paywall should sit where users want the forecast answer.
Reflection · Capstone

What I learned

Friction

The hard part

My first hunch was that conversion was dropping, but the data showed it had been flat at ~2% for 13 months. I rewrote my hypothesis around what the data actually showed.
Aha

The moment it clicked

Usage was going up but conversion didn’t move, which showed me FinWise had a value problem, not an engagement problem.
Takeaways

What stuck

Product experimentation and pricing models were new to me. The biggest lessons were to check the data before trusting a hunch and to test one clear bet at a time.
